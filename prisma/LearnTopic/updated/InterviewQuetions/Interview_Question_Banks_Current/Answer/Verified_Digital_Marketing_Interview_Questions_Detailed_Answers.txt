1.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is digital marketing?
    Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Digital marketing is the use of digital channels, platforms,
data, and technology to attract, engage, convert, and retain customers.
Common channels include search engines, paid advertising, social media,
email, content, websites, and other digital touchpoints.

A good digital marketing program connects channel activity to a business
objective. Instead of optimizing only clicks or impressions, a marketer
should understand the customer journey, define measurable goals, track
conversions, and evaluate business outcomes such as revenue, qualified
leads, customer acquisition cost, or retention.

Example : Example: An online retailer uses SEO to attract product-intent
searches, paid search to capture high-intent demand, email to re-engage
customers, and analytics to measure purchases and customer value.

Interview Tip : Give the definition first, then explain why it matters,
provide a practical example, and connect the concept to a measurable
marketing outcome.

What Interviewer Is Testing : Understanding of digital-marketing
fundamentals plus the ability to connect channels, metrics, customer
intent, experimentation, and business outcomes.

Key Points to Remember : Tie the concept to audience intent, funnel
stage, measurement, optimization, and business impact.

Common Follow-up Questions : Why would you choose this approach? What
metric would you use? What could cause the metric to be misleading? How
would you test your decision? What alternative would you consider?

Common Mistakes : Focusing only on clicks or impressions; confusing
related metrics; assuming correlation proves causation; ignoring
tracking quality; changing several variables at once; or giving a
channel recommendation without considering audience intent, economics,
and business objectives.

2.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : How do you categorise digital
    marketing? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Digital marketing can be categorized by the channel and
objective. Major categories include SEO, search engine marketing/PPC,
content marketing, social media marketing, email marketing, display
advertising, affiliate/influencer marketing, remarketing, and
analytics-driven lifecycle marketing.

Another useful classification is by funnel objective: awareness,
consideration, conversion, and retention. In practice, channels overlap.
For example, SEO can create awareness and conversions, while email can

5.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What are keywords? Level :
    Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Keywords are words or phrases that represent what users search
for in a search engine. In SEO and paid search, they help connect user
intent with relevant content or advertisements.

A marketer should consider search intent, relevance, search volume,
competition, commercial value, and the stage of the customer journey.
Modern SEO should focus on satisfying the underlying search intent
rather than mechanically repeating exact keywords.

Example : Example: For a laptop retailer, “buy business laptop”
indicates stronger commercial intent than a broad query such as “what is
a laptop.”

11. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : How do you calculate the
    return on investment of a marketing campaign? Level : Fresher /
    Entry-Level

Answer : A basic campaign ROI calculation is:

ROI = (Incremental Return − Campaign Cost) / Campaign Cost × 100

The difficult part is determining the incremental return attributable to
the campaign. Revenue should not automatically be treated as incremental
if the customer would have purchased without the campaign. A robust
analysis therefore considers attribution, margins, customer value, and
ideally controlled experiments or credible incrementality methods.

Example : Example: Build a small campaign, define the objective and
conversion event, establish a baseline, segment the results, identify
the largest bottleneck, test one change, and compare the post-test

14. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : How do you improve the
    conversion rate of a website? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Conversion-rate optimization (CRO) improves the percentage of
relevant visitors who complete a desired action. I would first identify
the conversion definition and funnel, analyze user behavior, locate
major drop-off points, inspect page speed and usability, and form
hypotheses.

Then I would test changes such as clearer value propositions, stronger
calls to action, simpler forms, better product information, trust
signals, or improved mobile UX. Results should be measured with
controlled experiments where feasible rather than assuming a change
caused improvement.

Example : Example: If 1,000 qualified visitors produce 40 purchases,
conversion rate is 4%. An A/B test might compare the current checkout
with a shorter version to determine whether friction is reducing
purchases.

provide a practical example, and connect the concept to a measurable

    PPC and CPC? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : PPC is the advertising model or campaign approach where payment
is commonly associated with clicks. CPC means cost per click—the amount
paid or incurred for each click under a CPC pricing model.

CPC is a metric/cost measure; PPC describes an advertising model. They
are related but not interchangeable terms.

Example : Example:

    Clicks = 1,000
    CPC = $2

24. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : How do you calculate ROI for a
    digital marketing campaign? Level : Intermediate

Answer : A basic campaign ROI calculation is:

ROI = (Incremental Return − Campaign Cost) / Campaign Cost × 100

The difficult part is determining the incremental return attributable to
the campaign. Revenue should not automatically be treated as incremental
if the customer would have purchased without the campaign. A robust
analysis therefore considers attribution, margins, customer value, and
ideally controlled experiments or credible incrementality methods.

Example : Example: A campaign costs $10,000 and produces $30,000 of
genuinely incremental contribution return. ROI = ($30,000 − $10,000) /
$10,000 × 100 = 200%. The exact business definition of “return” should
be stated.
