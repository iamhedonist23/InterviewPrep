1.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is the difference between
    raster and vector graphics? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Raster graphics are made from pixels, while vector graphics are
defined by mathematical paths, curves, fills, and strokes. Raster images
are resolution-dependent: enlarging them too much can make them appear
blurry or pixelated. Vector artwork can generally be scaled to different
sizes without losing geometric sharpness.

Raster is commonly used for photographs and detailed pixel-based
imagery. Vector is preferred for logos, icons, illustrations, and
artwork that must work across many sizes. In production, the choice
should be based on the content and output rather than assuming one
format is always better.

Example : Example: A company logo should normally be maintained as
vector artwork so it can be placed on a business card, website,
billboard, or sign without becoming pixelated. A photograph used in a
campaign is normally raster.

2.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : Explain the difference between
    RGB and CMYK, and when you use each. Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : RGB is an additive color model based on red, green, and blue
light and is used for screens and other digital displays. CMYK is a
subtractive printing model using cyan, magenta, yellow, and black inks.

A designer should work in the color space appropriate to the final
output. A screen design can use RGB, while a print project normally
follows the printer’s required CMYK workflow or color specification. RGB
colors may not have exact CMYK equivalents, so important brand colors
should be checked before production.

Example : Example: A website banner is designed in RGB for screen
display, while a printed brochure follows the printer’s specified CMYK
production workflow.

attention.

6.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is visual hierarchy?
    Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Visual hierarchy is the deliberate organization of visual
elements so viewers understand what to notice first, second, and later.
Designers create hierarchy through size, scale, contrast, color,
typography, spacing, position, and visual weight.

A strong hierarchy supports the intended user journey. If a promotional
design has a headline, supporting message, product image, and call to
action, the viewer should quickly understand the primary message and
what action to take.

7.  [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is white space, and why
    is it important? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : White space, or negative space, is the intentionally unoccupied
area around and between design elements. It does not have to be
literally white.

It improves readability, separates related and unrelated content,
creates visual breathing room, establishes hierarchy, and can make a
design feel more premium or focused. Removing whitespace simply to fit
more information can reduce clarity.

10. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is the difference between
    a logo, a brand identity and brand guidelines? Level : Fresher /
    Entry-Level

Answer : A logo is a recognizable visual mark or wordmark associated
with a brand. Brand identity is the broader visual system used to
express the brand, including logo usage, color, typography, imagery,
graphic elements, layouts, and other assets.

Brand guidelines document how those elements should be used
consistently. Therefore, a logo is one component of an identity, while
guidelines define rules for applying the identity consistently.

Example : Example: A designer uses the approved typefaces and color
palette but varies composition and imagery across a website banner,
social post, and print flyer so the work remains consistent without
becoming repetitive.

    Photoshop and Illustrator? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : Photoshop is primarily a raster/pixel-based image-editing and
compositing tool, while Illustrator is primarily a vector-graphics tool.

Photoshop is appropriate for tasks such as photo retouching, pixel-based
manipulation, and image compositing. Illustrator is appropriate for
logos, icons, vector illustrations, and artwork that needs to scale
cleanly. They can also be used together in the same workflow.

12. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is the difference between
    PNG, JPG, SVG and PDF? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : PNG is a raster format that supports lossless compression and
transparency, making it useful for graphics with transparency and web
assets. JPG/JPEG is a compressed raster format commonly used for
photographs and is generally smaller but lossy.

SVG is a vector format suited to scalable graphics such as icons and
logos, especially on the web. PDF is a document/container format that
can preserve text, vector artwork, raster images, fonts, and
print-production information depending on how it is created.

Example : Example: Use JPG for a photographic web image, PNG when
transparency is needed, SVG for a scalable web icon, and an appropriate
PDF for a print-ready document.

result. Connect design choices to audience, objective, brand, usability,

13. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What is DPI and why does it
    matter? Level : Fresher / Entry-Level

Answer : DPI means dots per inch and describes printer output density.
In design discussions it is often confused with PPI, which describes
pixel density/resolution of digital images.

For print, sufficient image resolution is important because a
low-resolution image may reproduce poorly at the intended physical size.
The correct resolution depends on the printing process, viewing
distance, image content, and printer specification; do not treat one DPI
number as a universal rule.

Example : Example: If a print image is intended to be reproduced at 10
inches wide, check its effective resolution at that physical size rather
than judging the source file only by its pixel dimensions.

15. [REPORTED / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : What are bleed and safe
    margins, and why are they important for print? Level : Fresher /
    Entry-Level

Answer : Bleed is extra artwork extending beyond the final trim edge so
small cutting variations do not create unwanted white edges. Safe
margins define an inner area where important text and elements should
remain so they are not too close to the trim or binding.

For example, a background color may extend into the bleed, while a
headline remains comfortably inside the safe area. Exact bleed and
margin requirements should come from the printer or production
specification.

64. [SCENARIO / SOURCE-LISTED] Question : How do you handle a situation
    where design quality, deadline and client requirements are pulling
    in different directions? Level : Advanced / Senior / Scenario-Based

Answer : Identify which requirements are non-negotiable, assess the risk
of reducing quality, and propose a prioritized plan. Protect critical
accessibility, brand, production, and correctness requirements while
identifying lower-value scope that can be deferred.

Communicate the trade-offs early and get explicit agreement on the
revised priority.

Example : Example: With one day left, prioritize the approved core
layout, correct dimensions, readable type, final assets, and production
checks; defer optional decorative variations until the critical
deliverable is safe.
